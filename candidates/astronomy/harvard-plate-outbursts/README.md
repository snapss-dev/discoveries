# Four more possible outbursts on Harvard plates

Status: **candidates**. Found by an agent in the DASCH light curves and plate images; not re-measured
independently, and the literature check is the agent's only. Produced with AI assistance; see the
repository README. Nothing has been reported to anyone.

These come from the same sweep that found the
[1905 outburst of V627 Peg](../../../new/astronomy/v627-peg-1905-outburst/README.md), which was
re-measured and is filed under `new/`.

## The sweep

A list of 1,057 known stars that brighten rarely and by a lot (WZ Sge-type dwarf novae, recurrent
novae, X-ray transients) was built from the AAVSO Variable Star Index. For 696 of them the DASCH DR7
light curve from the Harvard plates (about 1885 to 1990) was searched for clusters of detections well
above the star's normal level, and about 100 events were then examined on the plate images. Events
already in the literature were used as controls: the sweep recovered, among others, the 1900 eruption
of the recurrent nova V2487 Oph and the 1942 outburst of V627 Peg.

## The candidates

Magnitudes are DASCH pipeline values in B. "Normal" is the star's usual brightness.

| star | type | when | what the plates show | normal | agent's confidence |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| GY Cet | WZ Sge-type dwarf nova | 1943 June 16 to 29 | 9.9 on one plate; 11.9 thirteen days later on two plates from two telescopes; nothing 31 days after that | 18.5 | 0.7 |
| V624 Peg | WZ Sge-type dwarf nova | 1938 November 10 to 20 | 11.4 to 13.2 on six plates from three cameras; nothing 16 days before or 23 days after | 18.5 | 0.6 |
| V355 UMa | WZ Sge-type dwarf nova | 1930 December 12 to 1931 January 8 | 9.2 on one plate, 12.5 eighteen days later, three weaker images near the plate limit | 17.2 | 0.55 |
| V4641 Sgr | black-hole X-ray binary | 1901 May 21 to June 25 | 10.4 and 11.6 on plates from two telescopes, against a normal 13.6 | 13.6 | 0.55 |

The agent found no earlier report of any of the four. Its sources: the AAVSO index entry and revision
history for each star, and for GY Cet the index remarks (superoutbursts listed for 1999 and 2020 only),
for V355 UMa AAVSO Special Notice 233 and Kato et al. 2012 (the 2011 superoutburst described as the
first), for V624 Peg the 2011 discovery notices.

## Why these are only candidates

- **Not re-measured.** The V627 Peg event was measured a second time from the plate pixels with
  separate code. These four were not.
- **Few plates.** GY Cet rests on three plates at two epochs, and the brightest one carries a DASCH
  defect flag. V355 UMa rests on two convincing plates. V624 Peg was never caught brighter than 11.4,
  so it could be an ordinary outburst and not a superoutburst.
- **V4641 Sgr may be known.** The DASCH team's 2014 conference abstract (Grindlay et al., AAS meeting
  223, 406.06) says historical outbursts of this star were found on the plates, without dates. The 1901
  event may be one of them. The field is also crowded, and only two plates carry the case.
- **No second archive.** None was checked against plates from another observatory.

## A known gap in the method

The sweep only looks at detections that DASCH matched to a catalogued star at the target's position. A
detection that was never matched is missed: the 1898 outburst of MAXI J1820+070, which is in the
literature, was not recovered.

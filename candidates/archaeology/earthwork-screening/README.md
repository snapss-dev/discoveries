# Possible unrecorded earthworks from open lidar and satellite imagery: eight screens, no positions

Status: **candidates, none confirmed.** Positions, place names below the level of a county, state or
department, and images are withheld on purpose. Produced with AI assistance; see the repository README.
Nothing has been reported to any heritage body.

## What this page is

On 10 and 11 November 2026, AI agents screened open elevation models and satellite images in eight areas
for earthworks that are missing from the open registers. Each screen rendered known, registered sites
in the same way first, then looked for the same shapes elsewhere, then checked what it found against
the registers it could reach. This page sums up all eight. A ninth screen (Mexico) found nothing and has
its own page under dead-ends.

A warning that applies to every row: "not in the open register" is weaker than "unknown". Several
national registers are closed or incomplete online, and local archaeologists usually know more than any
open list.

## What was checked for novelty, and what was not

- Brazil (enclosure): the heritage institute's site layer (1829 points and 677 polygons in the region),
  the earthwork database of Peripato et al. (Science, 2023; 961 sites), OpenStreetMap (431 features) and
  Wikidata (160 items). Not checked: the institute's unpublished records.
- France: place names on current and historical IGN maps, OpenStreetMap, Wikidata. The national
  archaeological register (Patriarche) is not open, so this check cannot show that a site is unknown.
- Scotland: Canmore, the scheduled-monuments layer and the first-edition Ordnance Survey six-inch maps.
  Not checked: the council historic environment records.
- Norway: the national register through its open map service. Ireland: the bulk Sites and Monuments
  Record and the live Historic Environment Viewer service. Bavaria: the heritage office's monument layer.

## What the second pass did

It read every candidate file, log and calibration note of the eight screens, and looked again at the
evidence images for the Yonne ringwork and the Amazonas enclosure; both are as described. It did **not**
download the elevation data again or repeat the register queries. The sizes, counts and distances above
are the first pass's.

## What would settle each

A look by the body that keeps the register, then a field visit: the state superintendency of IPHAN in
Brazil; the regional archaeology service (SRA) of the DRAC in France; Historic Environment Scotland and
the council archaeology services; the county archaeologist in Norway; the National Monuments Service in
Ireland; the Bavarian State Office for the Preservation of Monuments. Positions are held back and can be
passed to those bodies.

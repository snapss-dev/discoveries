# Tjibangara, West Java, 22 May 1934: a meteorite fall the register may not have

Status: **candidate**. Produced with AI assistance; see the repository README.

## What was done

The full text of the Dutch newspaper archive [Delpher](https://www.delpher.nl), which also covers the
Dutch East Indies, was searched for reports of recovered meteorites: 7,325 articles from 1618 to 1995,
of which about 450 were read. The reports were compared with copies of the Meteoritical Bulletin
Database. The method recovered 15 registered falls (among them Uden, Utrecht, Ellemeet, Glanerbrug,
Tjabe, Djati-Pengilon, Rembang, Tjerebon and Banten), which shows it can find real ones.

## The reports

Two independent contemporary sources describe the same event.

- **Witness letters**, printed 26 May 1934 in *Het Nieuws van den Dag voor Nederlandsch-Indië*
  (Delpher `ddd:010230107:mpeg21:a0143`), from two estates near Soebang: on Tuesday 22 May at about
  9 in the morning, a rumble like thunder under a clear sky and a white, then green, object moving from
  north to south. 22 May 1934 was a Tuesday.
- **The Batavia observatory's analysis**, distributed by the Aneta news agency and printed in nine
  papers on 9 to 11 June 1934 (for example *Bataviaasch Nieuwsblad*, 9 June 1934, page 2, Delpher
  `ddd:011073695:mpeg21:a0103`): the fireball's path worked out from sightings between Batavia and
  Bandung, and a fragment of 45 g found at Tjibangara, about 27 km south-south-east of Tjiandjoer
  (Cianjur), roughly 7.04 S, 107.24 E. The observatory measured its density as 3.44, which fits a stony
  meteorite.

## It is not a known Java fall retold

| registered fall | date | why it is a different event |
|---|---|---|
| Banten | 24 May 1933 | A year earlier, 140 km away, an afternoon shower of stones sent to the volcanological service; a carbonaceous chondrite with density near 2.2 |
| Selakopi, Glanggang | 26 September 1939 | Five years later |

## Why it is only a candidate

- **The register check is by proxy.** No 1934 fall in Indonesia appears in NASA's copy of the official
  list (about 2013) or in a 2014 copy of the Bulletin's falls that includes doubtful and discredited
  entries. The live Meteoritical Bulletin Database refused automated access, as did the Wayback Machine,
  mindat, and the scanned catalogues of Hey and Grady. An entry under an unexpected name, or a doubtful
  entry without coordinates, is not excluded.
- **Thin physical evidence.** No finder is named and no petrographic description was printed: the
  fragment is tied to the fireball only by the observatory's statement.
- Nothing further appears in the newspapers after 27 June 1934.

## Who could settle it

The Meteoritical Society's Nomenclature Committee keeps the register. The stone went to the Batavia
observatory, whose successor is Indonesia's meteorological agency BMKG in Jakarta; Museum Geologi in
Bandung holds most historic Java meteorites; the observatory's annual report for 1934 would be the
primary document. Nothing has been sent to anyone.

# NASA G-LiHT lidar strips in Mexico outside the Maya area: no unrecorded platforms found

Status: **dead end** (searched, nothing found). The search was partial. Produced with AI assistance; see
the repository README.

## Why look

NASA's G-LiHT airborne lidar flew long, narrow strips across Mexico in 2013 for forest carbon work. In the
Maya lowlands those strips cross ancient settlement, and published studies have used them there. The
strips over central and southern Mexico outside the Maya area have had less attention.

## What was done

The 1 m terrain models of several 2013 flight campaigns in central and southern Mexico (the campaign names point to Guanajuato, Hidalgo, Veracruz, Morelos and Oaxaca)
were downloaded. A prefilter looked for raised, straight-sided platforms and grouped them into clusters;
the valid parts of the swept tiles were also rendered as shaded relief in 700 m chunks and looked at on
contact sheets. The rendering was first tried on a strip over a mapped Maya site in the Puuc region of
Yucatan.

## Result

No candidate was kept. One cluster was examined closely, checked against OpenStreetMap and Wikidata,
and dropped.

## Coverage

About 27 percent of the downloaded strips were swept before time ran out, so this is a weak negative.
The strips are only a few hundred metres wide, which makes a structure hard to judge without its
surroundings.

## What the second pass did

It read the first pass's scripts, file lists and detection tables. It did not repeat the sweep, and it
did not re-derive the 27 percent figure.

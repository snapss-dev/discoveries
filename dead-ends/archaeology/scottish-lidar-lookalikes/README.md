# Five lidar suspects in Scotland that were not sites

Status: **dead ends**. Produced with AI assistance; see the repository README.

The search described under
[two possible cairns in Galloway](../../../candidates/archaeology/galloway-cairns/README.md) threw up
round mounds and ring banks that looked promising in the relief images and failed a simple check. They
are kept here because the same look-alikes will come up in any lidar search for cairns.

| where | what it looked like | what it was | what gave it away |
|---|---|---|---|
| Kintyre | a ring-shaped hollow with a raised rim, like a ring cairn or small enclosure | a modern forestry quarry | the current map shows the quarry |
| Galloway | a round mound with a hollowed top | a gravel pit | the 1860s Ordnance Survey six-inch map labels it "Gravel Pit" |
| Kintyre | a steep round mound 9 m high | a natural rock knoll | drawn with hachures as a natural feature on the 1860s map |
| Angus | a cluster of small mounds in an arable field | most likely temporary heaps on the day of the lidar flight | no older survey covers the field to compare |
| Galloway | a mound 8 m across on moorland | most likely a clump of gorse or scrub left in the terrain model | it sits among scattered shrubs on the air photograph |

## Lesson

Look at the oldest large-scale map and the current map before anything else. Three of the five were
settled in a minute by a map label.

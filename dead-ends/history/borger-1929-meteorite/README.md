# Borger, Drenthe, 1929: a reported meteorite that was a weathered boulder

Status: **killed**. Produced with AI assistance; see the repository README.

## The claim

A search of the Dutch newspaper archive [Delpher](https://www.delpher.nl) for unregistered meteorite
falls turned up local reports of 1, 8 and 15 October 1929: a 300 g stone picked up beside the road from
Borger to Rolde after a fireball, which a Groningen mineralogist was said to have called a genuine
meteorite. No such fall is in the Meteoritical Bulletin.

## What killed it

A follow-up in the same archive. *De Noord-Ooster*, 17 October 1929 (Delpher
`MMVEEN01:000082703:mpeg21:a0005`), repeated in the *Winschoter Courant* two days later, reports that it
was "geen meteoriet, doch een sterk verweerde Drentsche kei. Er groeien zelfs korstmossen op": not a
meteorite but a strongly weathered local boulder, with lichens growing on it.

The first reports already had the classic marks of a false alarm (a glowing ball on the ground, cooled in
water), and a 1947 Dutch overview of meteorites leaves the event out.

## Also set aside

- Leende 1939: orange-yellow round stones, probably ironstone nodules.
- Rotterdam 1946: sulphur-yellow outside, no fusion crust.
- Wedde 1852, Tjoeroet 1883, St. Michielsgestel 1853, Gouda 1927, Den Haag 1885: each refuted by a later
  article in the same archive.

## Lesson

Search the later dates for the same place and names before believing a first report. The one report from
this search that survived that test is
[Tjibangara, 1934](../../../candidates/history/tjibangara-1934-meteorite/README.md).

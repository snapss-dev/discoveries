# A 1934 meteorite fall in West Java that the register may not have, and one dead end

Status: Tjibangara 1934 is a **candidate**. Borger 1929 is **killed**. Produced with AI assistance;
see the repository README.

## What was done

The full text of the Dutch newspaper archive [Delpher](https://www.delpher.nl), which also covers the
Dutch East Indies, was searched for reports of recovered meteorites: 7,325 articles from 1618 to 1995,
of which about 450 were read. The reports were compared with copies of the Meteoritical Bulletin
Database. The method recovered 15 registered falls (among them Uden, Utrecht, Ellemeet, Glanerbrug,
Tjabe, Djati-Pengilon, Rembang, Tjerebon and Banten), which shows it can find real ones.

## Tjibangara, West Java, 22 May 1934: candidate

Two independent contemporary sources describe the same event.

- **Witness letters**, printed 26 May 1934 in *Het Nieuws van den Dag voor Nederlandsch-Indië*
  (Delpher `ddd:010230107:mpeg21:a0143`), from two estates near Soebang: on Tuesday 22 May at about
  9 in the morning, a rumble like thunder under a clear sky and a white, then green, object moving from
  north to south. 22 May 1934 was a Tuesday.
- **The Batavia observatory's analysis**, distributed by the Aneta news agency and printed in nine
  papers on 9 to 11 June 1934 (for example *Bataviaasch Nieuwsblad*, 9 June 1934, page 2, Delpher
  `ddd:011073695:mpeg21:a0103`): the fireball's path worked out from sightings between Batavia and
  Bandung, and a fragment of 45 g found at Tjibangara, about 27 km south-south-east of Tjiandjoer
  (Cianjur), roughly 7.04 S, 107.24 E. The observatory measured its density as 3.44, which fits a stony
  meteorite.

**It is not a known Java fall retold.**

| registered fall | date | why it is a different event |
|---|---|---|
| Banten | 24 May 1933 | A year earlier, 140 km away, an afternoon shower of stones sent to the volcanological service; a carbonaceous chondrite with density near 2.2 |
| Selakopi, Glanggang | 26 September 1939 | Five years later |

**Register check, and its limit.** No 1934 fall in Indonesia appears in NASA's copy of the official list
(about 2013) or in a 2014 copy of the Bulletin's falls that includes doubtful and discredited entries.
The live Meteoritical Bulletin Database refused automated access, as did the Wayback Machine, mindat,
and the scanned catalogues of Hey and Grady. So an entry under an unexpected name, or a doubtful entry
without coordinates, is not excluded. This is why the status is "candidate".

**Other weak points.** No finder is named, and no petrographic description was printed: the fragment is
tied to the fireball only by the observatory's statement. Nothing further appears in the newspapers
after 27 June 1934.

**Where the stone might be.** It went to the Batavia observatory, whose successor is Indonesia's
meteorological agency BMKG in Jakarta. Museum Geologi in Bandung holds most historic Java meteorites.
The observatory's annual report for 1934 would be the primary document.

## Borger to Rolde road, Drenthe, 1929: killed

Local papers of 1, 8 and 15 October 1929 reported a 300 g stone picked up after a fireball and said that
a Groningen mineralogist had called it a genuine meteorite. The follow-up settles it: *De Noord-Ooster*,
17 October 1929 (Delpher `MMVEEN01:000082703:mpeg21:a0005`), reports that it was "geen meteoriet, doch
een sterk verweerde Drentsche kei. Er groeien zelfs korstmossen op": not a meteorite but a strongly
weathered local boulder, with lichens growing on it. The first reports had the classic marks of a false
alarm (a glowing ball cooled in water), and the register's doubtful list for the Netherlands does not
include it.

Two weaker reports were also set aside: Leende 1939 (orange-yellow round stones, probably ironstone
nodules) and Rotterdam 1946 (sulphur-yellow outside, no fusion crust). Five more were refuted by later
articles in the same archive.

## Who could settle the candidate

The Meteoritical Society's Nomenclature Committee keeps the register. A curator at BMKG, Museum Geologi
Bandung or Naturalis (Leiden) could say whether the specimen survives. Nothing has been sent to anyone.

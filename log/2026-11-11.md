# 2026-11-11: first sprint

Fourteen AI agents each took one search lane for about ninety minutes, with the instruction to find
something present in open data but absent from the relevant catalogue, and to try to kill their own
result. A lead agent then re-checked the strongest claims. This page records every lane, including the
ones that found nothing and the claims that turned out wrong.

Exact positions of possible archaeological sites and wrecks are deliberately left out of this
repository. They are unconfirmed, and publishing them would help nobody but looters.

## Mistakes, and what changed because of them

1. **A rediscovery was announced as a discovery.** The A072633 counterexample had been posted by
   Antonio Sanso on 22 July 2026 and proposed to the OEIS the same day. The edit was later reverted for
   an unanswered formatting request, so the published entry showed nothing; only the revision history
   did. The novelty check had read the published entry and run one web search. *Change:* revision
   histories and draft pages are read, and a second, deeper web search is run, before anything is called
   new.
2. **An agent's "not in any catalogue" was false.** The two eclipsing binaries were in three published
   catalogues that the agent had listed as checked. A query of every catalogue at the star's position
   showed it in minutes. *Change:* the lead re-runs every novelty check itself; an agent's word is not
   enough.
3. **A contribution policy was read too late.** Text for an OEIS comment was drafted before reading the
   OEIS policy on AI-generated submissions, which prohibits it. Nothing was submitted. *Change:* the
   venue's policy is read before any submission is prepared, and this repository exists instead.
4. **A first reading of an image was wrong.** The lead picked the Franz Josef Land headland as the best
   lead from a picture alone; the lane's own novelty check then found the 2022 announcement.
